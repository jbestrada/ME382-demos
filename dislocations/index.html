How does an edge dislocation move?

Advance the dislocation one lattice spacing at a time. Watch the distorted lattice, the terminating extra half-plane, and the Burgers circuit move together along the slip plane.

Model note: This is a schematic 2D triangular lattice driven by a regularized elastic displacement field—not a bubble-raft experiment or molecular-dynamics simulation. The slider shows discrete glide positions; the field is softened near the core so the ideal elastic singularity is finite.

What moves?

The core is where the lattice distortion is largest. In this schematic, five- and seven-neighbor sites mark the local coordination change around the core. Advancing the slider moves that distortion by one lattice spacing.

Glide plane & half-plane

The gold dashed horizontal line is the glide (slip) plane. The blue dashed line is the extra half-plane, ending at the core. As the edge dislocation glides, the extra half-plane's termination moves along the crystal.

Burgers circuit

Trace the dashed path around the core. Its endpoints do not meet: the closure gap is the Burgers vector, shown as b. Here it is parallel to the glide plane and has a magnitude of one lattice spacing.
